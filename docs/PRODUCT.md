# YaadOM: why it exists

## The person we build for first

Someone who writes down a few short things they want to keep in mind and then gets on with life.
They will not open the app every day, will not tick anything off and will not tune settings.
If YaadOM only works for people who show up and answer, it fails them.

**Goal for everyone:** familiarity through repeated, calm exposure. Seeing a line many times is the product.
**Goal for proactive people (later, opt-in):** active recall, such as hiding the answer and checking yourself.

## Principles

1. **Zero-effort path first.** Writing one line is the whole setup. Defaults (7:00 to 22:00, about once an hour) are already on.
2. **The reminder is complete on its own.** Reading the notification is enough. Nothing to tap, answer or clear.
3. **Protect goodwill.** Quiet notifications (no sound, no vibration), a small handful per reminder, a Pause button, and no guilt words (due, overdue, streak, missed).
4. **Keep it fresh.** Points take turns, a few at a time, and times shift by a minute or two so it never feels like a clock.
5. **Show up where people already look.** The lock screen today; widgets and voice are candidates for later.
6. **Cheap adding is a hypothesis.** One-step adding is in, but whether it helps or leads to clutter needs testing with real people.
7. **Smart features are optional.** Plain repetition always works without them.

## The biggest risk

People uninstall when it feels like a chore. So the app never asks for upkeep, holds at most 15 Daily and 15 Long-term points (a calm limit, not a quota), and slowly becomes something to enjoy: **Your sky** appears after your points have come back to you 30 times. Each point is a star that grows each time it comes back and never dims.

## Not in this first version

Voice, widgets, lock-screen tricks, routine moments, a tap-to-recall mode, sharing and premium. Each has to earn its place against the principles above.
